## Best-supported cause
**EU-1 payment-gateway degradation is the best-supported trigger; v18’s longer timeout and higher retry allowance amplified its effect on checkout duration.** The evidence goes beyond timing alone: slow checkout traces show most of the request spent in authorization, with multiple timed-out attempts against `paygw-eu-1`; the provider independently identified faulty acquirer connection-pool nodes in that region. [traces-2026-10-06:L380-L384] [provider-status:L7-L9] v2.14.0/profile v18 was already active while latency remained normal through 14:18, so the release by itself does not explain the onset; its retry/timeout behavior plausibly explains why gateway degradation produced especially long requests. [checkout-metrics:L126-L140] [config-diff:L35-L44]

## Competing explanation
**Orders-db reindex/CPU is a plausible concurrent contributor, but the evidence does not support it as the primary cause of the multi-second delays.** Although CPU was elevated during the reindex, DB query p95 remained about 24–30ms and pool-wait p95 about 1–3ms across the incident window. [db-metrics:L1] [db-metrics:L132-L163] The sampled slow traces show DB spans of only 17–28ms while payment authorization consumed 8.659–26.164 seconds. [traces-2026-10-06:L369-L375] [traces-2026-10-06:L377-L385] Checkout was still very slow at 14:41, when DB CPU had fallen to 26%. [db-metrics:L163] [checkout-metrics:L163] Thus the reindex may have added load, but it does not fit the observed dominant delay or its persistence after the reindex ended.

## Recommended mitigation
The incident is reported resolved without action, so **make no change now**. If the degradation recurs, the smallest reversible runbook option is for the incident commander to roll back only the runtime payment-client profile from v18 to v17, rather than rolling back the application release or changing the database. The runbook says this config rollback is fully reversible and applies within about 60 seconds. [runbook-payments:L12-L15] Preserve fraud screening, its `hold_for_review` timeout behavior, and idempotency-key reuse; these are explicit safety requirements. [runbook-payments:L3-L8] After any approved mitigation, monitor checkout p95 and payment-client error ratio for 15 minutes and check reconciliation for duplicate charges. [runbook-payments:L19-L22]

## Uncertainty
The traces are samples, not a complete accounting of all slow checkouts, and the provider’s impact times are approximate. The evidence strongly supports gateway authorization delays as the dominant mechanism in the sampled slow requests, but cannot quantify how much v18 increased the total incident-wide latency or exclude smaller contributions from DB load or other factors.
